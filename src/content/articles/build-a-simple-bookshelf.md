A basic bookshelf is a great step up from smaller projects — it introduces dado joints (or pocket-hole alternatives) and a larger-scale assembly while still being achievable for a confident beginner.

## What You'll Need

- Two side panels, a top, a bottom, and several shelf boards (plywood or solid lumber)
- A back panel (thin plywood works well) for stability
- Wood glue, screws or pocket-hole screws
- Sandpaper, a square, clamps

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Cut all panels to size**, double-checking that the two side panels are identical in length.
2. **Mark shelf positions** on the inside of both side panels, using a square to keep marks level and matching on each side.
3. **Cut dado grooves for shelves** (or use pocket-hole joinery as a simpler alternative) at your marked positions.
4. **Dry-fit the whole frame** before gluing — check that everything sits square and level.
5. **Glue and assemble the frame**, clamping while checking diagonals for square.
6. **Attach the back panel.** A full plywood back significantly improves rigidity and helps keep the whole unit square over time.
7. **Sand and finish** once assembly is complete and glue has fully cured.

## Common Mistakes

- Skipping the back panel, resulting in a bookshelf that racks (leans) out of square once loaded with books.
- Mismatched shelf marks between the two side panels, leading to shelves that aren't level.
- Underestimating shelf sag — thinner shelf boards can bow under the weight of books over long spans; keep spans reasonable or use thicker material.

## Taking It Further

Once you're comfortable with this basic build, adding adjustable shelf pins, face frames, or trim details is a natural next step. A structured woodworking plans library can provide exact measurements and joinery diagrams for more advanced bookshelf and cabinet variations.
