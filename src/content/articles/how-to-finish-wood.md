Finishing protects your project and determines much of its final look — choosing the right approach (and applying it well) makes a bigger difference than most beginners expect.

## Your Main Options

1. **Stain.** Enhances the wood's natural grain and color without hiding it. Best for higher-quality wood you want to showcase, like oak or walnut furniture pieces.
2. **Paint.** Fully covers the wood, hiding grain and imperfections — a practical choice for budget woods like pine, or projects where color matters more than grain.
3. **Clear sealant (polyurethane, lacquer, oil finishes).** Protects the wood while keeping its natural, unstained appearance — often used alone or as a topcoat over stain.

## How to Choose

1. **Consider the wood quality.** Nicer, more attractive grain (oak, walnut, cherry) is usually worth showcasing with stain or a clear finish rather than covering with paint.
2. **Think about the project's use.** High-touch or outdoor pieces need a durable topcoat regardless of stain or paint choice — bare stain alone isn't enough protection long-term.
3. **Match your skill level.** Paint is more forgiving of minor sanding inconsistencies than stain, which can highlight uneven sanding or blotchy absorption, especially on softwoods like pine.

## Application Basics

1. **Sand thoroughly first** (see our [sanding guide](../how-to-sand-wood-the-right-way/)) — finish reveals sanding mistakes rather than hiding them.
2. **Apply thin, even coats.** Multiple thin coats produce better results than one thick, uneven one, for both stain and clear finishes.
3. **Sand lightly between coats** of clear finish (not stain) to remove dust nibs and improve adhesion of the next layer.
4. **Let each coat fully cure** before handling or applying the next, following the product's recommended time.

## Common Mistakes

- Skipping a pre-stain conditioner on blotch-prone woods like pine, leading to uneven color.
- Applying finish in a dusty or humid environment, trapping debris or moisture in the coat.
- Under-protecting outdoor or high-use pieces with a finish rated only for light indoor use.

## Getting Consistent Results

Finishing technique improves quickly with a few completed projects. A structured woodworking plans library typically recommends a specific finish for each build based on the wood and intended use, which is a reliable way to get consistent results while you build your own judgment.
