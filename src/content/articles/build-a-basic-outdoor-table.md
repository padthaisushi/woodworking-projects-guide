An outdoor table introduces a full leg-and-frame structure, plus weather considerations that indoor furniture doesn't need to account for.

## Wood and Hardware Considerations

Choose a rot-resistant wood like cedar or pressure-treated lumber, and use exterior-rated wood glue and stainless or coated screws to prevent rust staining over time.

## What You'll Need

- Four legs, an apron frame (front, back, two sides), and tabletop boards
- Exterior wood glue, exterior-rated screws
- Sandpaper, a square, clamps, exterior finish or sealant

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Cut legs and apron pieces to size**, keeping all four legs identical in length for a level table.
2. **Assemble the apron frame** using pocket-hole joinery or corner brackets, checking square with diagonal measurements.
3. **Attach the legs to the apron frame**, using glue and screws (or bolts for extra strength and future disassembly if needed).
4. **Add a center support** for wider tabletops to prevent sagging or warping over time.
5. **Cut and attach tabletop boards**, leaving small consistent gaps between boards to allow for water drainage and wood movement.
6. **Sand all surfaces**, including edges and the underside.
7. **Apply an exterior-rated finish**, and plan to reapply periodically based on sun and weather exposure.

## Common Mistakes

- Using interior-rated glue or hardware, leading to joint failure or rust staining after exposure to rain.
- Attaching tabletop boards with no gap, which can cause warping or cracking as the wood expands and contracts with moisture.
- Skipping a finish reapplication schedule, letting UV and moisture degrade the wood faster than necessary.

## Building Toward More Advanced Outdoor Furniture

Once this build is solid, benches, planters, and larger outdoor sets use many of the same principles at different scales. A structured woodworking plans library typically includes a full outdoor furniture set with matching joinery and finish specifications.
