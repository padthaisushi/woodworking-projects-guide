Woodworking is a safe hobby overall, but a few consistent habits make the difference between a minor scare and a serious injury — especially once power tools are involved.

## Essential Safety Habits

1. **Wear eye and ear protection.** Safety glasses protect against flying debris on nearly every cut; hearing protection matters more than most beginners realize with prolonged power tool use.
2. **Avoid loose clothing and jewelry.** Anything that can catch in a spinning blade or bit is a real hazard — roll up sleeves and remove dangling jewelry before working.
3. **Use push sticks and guards.** Keep hands a safe distance from blades using push sticks, push blocks, or featherboards rather than guiding stock by hand near a cutting edge.
4. **Unplug tools before changing blades or bits.** A disconnected power source removes the risk of accidental activation during adjustments.
5. **Keep your workspace clear and well-lit.** Clutter and poor lighting are common contributors to trips, missteps, and misjudged cuts.
6. **Know your tool before you use it.** Read the manual and understand the specific hazards of each new tool before your first use, rather than learning by trial and error.

## Dust and Long-Term Health

Wood dust exposure adds up over time. Use dust collection where possible, wear a proper dust mask (not just a thin paper one) during sanding and cutting, and ventilate your workspace.

## Common Mistakes

- Removing blade guards "to see better," which removes a key safety barrier.
- Working while fatigued or rushed, when most tool accidents tend to happen.
- Skipping hearing protection because power tool use feels "occasional," when cumulative exposure still matters.

## Building Safe Habits From the Start

Safety habits are much easier to build correctly from your very first project than to retrofit later. A structured woodworking plans library often includes safety notes specific to each build's tools and techniques, reinforcing good habits project by project.
