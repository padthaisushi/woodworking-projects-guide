A phone stand is one of the smallest useful things you can make from wood, and it is a good way to turn the short 2x4 offcuts left over from bigger builds into something you use every day — or give away. This design is a single block with one angled slot. There is no glue, no screws, and nothing to assemble, so it is a gentle first project for practicing accurate marking and saw cuts.

The dimensions below assume a standard 2x4, which actually measures about 1-1/2" x 3-1/2". If your scrap is a different size, the method is the same; just keep the proportions.

## Finished Size

- About 5" wide, 3-1/2" deep, and 1-1/2" tall
- A slot about 3/4" deep, tilted back roughly 15–20 degrees, sized to your phone in its case

## What You'll Need

**Wood**

- One clear (knot-free) 2x4 offcut at least 6" long, **untreated**. A hardwood scrap of similar size looks nicer and is heavier, which helps stability.

**Supplies**

- Sandpaper: 80, 120, and 180 grit
- A finish that is pleasant to handle once cured, such as a wipe-on oil or water-based polyurethane (optional)
- Four small felt or cork pads for the bottom (optional)

**Tools**

- Hand saw (a fine-toothed pull saw or backsaw is easiest for the slot)
- Chisel, 1/4" to 1/2" wide, and a mallet or hammer
- Combination square or speed square, pencil, and a sliding bevel or protractor
- Clamp or vise
- Optional: drill with a 5/8" bit for a charging-cable hole
- Safety glasses

## Step 1: Measure Your Phone First

Put the phone in the case you actually use and measure its thickness at the thickest point, usually across the camera bump if that end goes into the slot, or across the body if it doesn't. Add about 1/16". That number is your slot width. For many phones in slim cases it lands between 7/16" and 1/2".

## Step 2: Cut the Block to Length

Square a line across the 2x4 at 5" from a clean end and cut it off. Choose a section without knots near where the slot will go — knots are hard to chisel and can crack out. If you are new to marking, our guide to [measuring and marking wood accurately](../how-to-measure-and-mark-wood/) covers how to keep the line square on every face.

## Step 3: Lay Out the Slot

Lay the block on its wide face so it sits 1-1/2" tall.

1. On the top face, draw a line across the full 5" width, **1-1/4" back from the front edge**. This is the front wall of the slot.
2. Draw a second line behind it, one slot width away (for example, 1/2").
3. Set a sliding bevel or protractor to **15–20 degrees off vertical**, leaning toward the back. On both end faces, carry each line down from the top at that angle.
4. Mark a depth line on both end faces **3/4" below the top**. Do not go deeper than about half the block's height, or the thin wood behind the slot can snap off.

Shade the waste between the lines so you do not cut on the wrong side.

## Step 4: Saw the Slot Walls

Clamp the block firmly with one end face toward you. Saw on the **waste side** of each line, following the angle on both end faces at the same time, and stop exactly at the depth line. Sawing a little from each end and checking both sides keeps the cut from wandering. Make a few extra saw cuts in the waste between the two walls — this makes chiseling much easier.

## Step 5: Chisel Out the Waste

With the block still clamped, chop out the waste between the saw cuts with the chisel, working in from each end toward the middle. Keep the bevel of the chisel facing the waste and take light cuts as you get close to the depth line. Pare the bottom of the slot flat so the phone sits level.

Test the fit with your phone. If it is tight, pare a thin shaving off the **back** wall only, so the front wall stays crisp.

## Step 6: Optional Charging-Cable Hole

If you want to charge the phone while it stands, drill a 5/8" hole straight through from the back face of the block so that it meets the center of the slot bottom. Check where your phone's charging port sits and how big your cable's plug is before you drill — some plugs need a larger hole or a notch cut down from the slot instead.

## Step 7: Shape and Sand

Ease all the sharp edges with 80 grit, then smooth everything with 120 and 180. Rounding the front top edge a little makes the stand feel better in the hand and keeps it from catching a sleeve. Our [sanding guide](../how-to-sand-wood-the-right-way/) explains why skipping grits leaves scratches that show up under a finish.

## Step 8: Finish

The stand can be left bare, but a finish keeps it clean when it is handled every day. A wipe-on oil gives a natural look, and water-based polyurethane is more durable and does not yellow pine much. Let the finish cure fully according to the label before setting a phone in it, or the case can stick or pick up marks. See [how to finish wood](../how-to-finish-wood/) for the trade-offs. Add felt or cork pads underneath so it does not scratch the desk.

## Safety Notes

- Wear safety glasses while sawing and chiseling — small chips fly when chopping across the grain.
- **Clamp the block. Never hold it in your hand while chiseling.** Keep both hands behind the cutting edge and push the chisel away from your body.
- A small block is easy to lose control of on a power saw. If you cut the blank on a miter saw or table saw, start from a longer board and cut the 5" piece off the end, rather than trimming a tiny piece.
- Wear a dust mask when sanding, and dispose of oil-soaked rags as the finish label directs, because some oils can heat up and catch fire in a crumpled pile.
- New to tools? Read our [woodworking safety tips for beginners](../woodworking-safety-tips-for-beginners/) first.

## Common Mistakes

- **Measuring the phone without its case.** The slot ends up too narrow for the way the phone is actually used.
- **Cutting the slot too deep.** Deeper is not more secure; it weakens the back of the block. About half the block height is plenty.
- **Sawing on the line instead of beside it.** The slot comes out a saw-kerf too wide. Always leave the line on the part you keep.
- **Using a knotty piece.** Knots tear out when chiseled and can leave a hole in the slot wall.
- **Putting the slot too close to the back.** The phone's weight then sits behind the base and the stand tips backward. Keep more wood behind the slot than in front of it.

## Simple Variations

- **Two-device stand:** Make the block 8" wide and cut two slots of different widths, one for a phone and one for an e-reader.
- **Business-card or photo holder:** The same block with a narrow 1/8" saw-kerf slot holds cards, place cards, or small prints.
- **Hardwood gift version:** A walnut, cherry, or oak offcut with an oil finish looks more finished as a present, and the extra weight makes it steadier.
