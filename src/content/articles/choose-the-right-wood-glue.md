Most woodworking glue-ups fail from either the wrong glue choice or poor clamping technique, not the wood itself. Here's how to pick the right adhesive.

## Common Wood Glue Types

1. **PVA (yellow) glue.** The standard choice for most indoor woodworking — strong, easy to use, and sands well once dry. Not fully waterproof.
2. **Polyurethane glue.** Waterproof and gap-filling, good for outdoor projects, but expands as it cures and requires careful clamping.
3. **Epoxy.** Very strong and gap-filling, ideal for uneven joints or mixed materials, but slower to cure and more expensive.
4. **Cyanoacrylate (CA) glue.** Fast-setting, useful for small repairs or holding pieces in place temporarily, but not typically a primary structural adhesive for larger joints.

## How to Choose

1. **Indoor furniture and boxes** — standard PVA glue is almost always the right, cost-effective choice.
2. **Outdoor projects** (planters, birdhouses, garden furniture) — use a waterproof polyurethane or exterior-rated PVA glue.
3. **Uneven or gap-heavy joints** — epoxy fills gaps better than PVA and holds strength even when the fit isn't perfect.
4. **Quick tack or small repairs** — CA glue is fast and convenient, though usually a supplement rather than your primary glue.

## Application Tips

- Apply an even, thin layer to both mating surfaces for the strongest bond.
- Clamp with enough pressure to see slight glue squeeze-out, then wipe excess immediately with a damp cloth before it dries.
- Respect the glue's full cure time (often 24 hours) before stressing the joint, even if it feels solid sooner.

## Common Mistakes

- Using standard PVA glue on an outdoor project, leading to joint failure after rain exposure.
- Under-clamping, which is one of the most common reasons glue joints fail even with the right adhesive.
- Not wiping excess glue before it dries, which can block stain absorption and leave visible blotches.

## Getting Glue-Ups Right Every Time

Glue choice is one of those small decisions that has an outsized effect on how long a project lasts. A structured woodworking plans library typically specifies the right glue type for each build, removing the guesswork from this step.
