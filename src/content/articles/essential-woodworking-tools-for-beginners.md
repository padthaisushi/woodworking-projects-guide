It's easy to overspend on tools before you know what you actually need. Here's a realistic starting toolkit that covers the vast majority of beginner projects.

## The Core Toolkit

1. **A measuring tape and combination square.** Accurate measuring and marking matters more than any other single factor in clean results.
2. **A saw.** A hand saw works for many beginner projects; a circular saw or jigsaw speeds things up significantly once you're ready to invest.
3. **A drill/driver.** Covers pilot holes, screwing, and many basic joinery techniques.
4. **Clamps.** At least two or three — essential for glue-ups and holding pieces steady while cutting or drilling.
5. **Sandpaper in a few grits.** 120 for shaping and smoothing, 220 for a finished surface.
6. **A basic chisel set.** Useful for cleaning up joints and small detail work even in simple projects.

## Nice-to-Haves (Add Later)

- A random orbital sander, once hand-sanding starts feeling slow.
- A router, for edge profiles and more advanced joinery.
- A table saw or miter saw, once you're doing enough repeated cuts to justify the investment.

## How to Prioritize Purchases

1. **Buy based on your next project, not a hypothetical future one.** Add tools as specific projects require them rather than stocking up all at once.
2. **Prioritize accuracy tools first.** A good square and measuring tape prevent more frustration than any power tool will fix.
3. **Consider quality over quantity for cutting tools.** A single good saw blade or chisel set outperforms a cheap multi-piece kit.

## Common Mistakes

- Buying a large tool set before knowing which tools you'll actually reach for.
- Skipping clamps, which are cheap but make an enormous difference in glue-up quality.
- Underinvesting in sandpaper variety, which affects the finished look more than most people expect.

## Building Your Toolkit Around Real Projects

The fastest way to figure out what tools you actually need is working through a few real, guided projects. A structured woodworking plans library will typically list the exact tools required for each build, so you invest only in what you'll use.
