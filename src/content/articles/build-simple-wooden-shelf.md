A simple wooden shelf is one of the best first projects in woodworking — it teaches measuring, cutting, sanding, and finishing without requiring advanced joinery or expensive tools.

## What You'll Need

- One 1x10 or 1x12 board (pine or poplar works well for beginners)
- Wood screws or shelf brackets
- Sandpaper (120 and 220 grit)
- Wood glue (optional, for reinforced joints)
- A saw (hand saw or circular saw), drill, level

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Decide on dimensions.** Measure the space where the shelf will go, and cut your board to length, leaving a little clearance on each side.
2. **Sand the board.** Start with 120 grit to smooth rough edges, then finish with 220 grit for a clean surface ready for stain or paint.
3. **Mark bracket or ledger positions.** Use a level to mark a straight, even line where your brackets or ledger strip will attach to the wall.
4. **Install brackets or a ledger strip.** Screw brackets into wall studs (or use appropriate wall anchors), always double-checking level before fully tightening.
5. **Attach the shelf.** Set the board onto the brackets and secure it with screws from underneath, or glue and clamp if using a ledger-strip method.
6. **Finish the wood.** Apply stain, paint, or a clear sealant once everything is dry-fit and sanded to your liking.

## Common Mistakes

- Skipping a stud finder and relying only on wall anchors for a heavier shelf.
- Not checking level before final attachment, resulting in a visibly tilted shelf.
- Rushing the sanding step, which shows clearly once stain is applied.

## Why This Project Is a Great Starting Point

Building a simple shelf teaches the core skills — accurate measuring, clean cuts, sanding, and finishing — that carry over into nearly every other woodworking project.
