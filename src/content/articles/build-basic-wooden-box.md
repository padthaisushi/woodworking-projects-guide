A basic wooden box is a compact project that introduces squaring, joining, and finishing in a small, forgiving format — perfect before moving on to larger furniture builds.

## What You'll Need

- Four side pieces cut to matching dimensions, plus a bottom panel
- Wood glue and clamps (or brad nails for extra hold)
- Sandpaper (120 and 220 grit)
- A square for checking corners
- Saw, drill or brad nailer

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Cut your pieces to size.** Measure twice and cut four side panels of matching height and appropriate lengths for your box footprint, plus a bottom panel sized to fit inside the sides.
2. **Dry-fit before gluing.** Assemble the box without glue first to check that corners are square and pieces fit cleanly.
3. **Glue and clamp the sides.** Apply wood glue to the mating edges, assemble the four sides into a frame, and clamp until square — checking diagonals are equal to confirm squareness.
4. **Attach the bottom.** Once the frame is dry, glue and/or nail the bottom panel into place.
5. **Sand all surfaces.** Work through 120 then 220 grit, paying extra attention to corners and glue squeeze-out.
6. **Finish as desired.** Stain, paint, or seal depending on the box's intended use.

## Common Mistakes

- Not checking for square before the glue sets, resulting in a lopsided box.
- Using too much glue, which squeezes out and can interfere with staining later if not cleaned up while wet.
- Skipping clamps and relying only on hand pressure, leading to weak joints.

## Where to Go From Here

Once you're comfortable with basic butt-joint boxes, the natural next step is learning simple joinery (like rabbets or dadoes) for stronger, cleaner-looking builds. A structured woodworking plans library can walk you through that progression with clear, tested plans rather than trial and error.
