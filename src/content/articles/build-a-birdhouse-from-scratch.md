A birdhouse is a small, rewarding project that introduces angled cuts and functional design — plus you get a finished piece that serves a real purpose in your yard.

## Basic Design Considerations

Entrance hole size and box dimensions vary by bird species, so it's worth a quick check on what you're hoping to attract (many general songbirds do well with a 1.5-inch entrance hole and roughly a 5x5 inch floor). Ventilation gaps and drainage holes in the floor are essential regardless of species.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

1. **Cut your panels.** You'll need a front, back, two sides, a floor, and a roof (often angled for water runoff) — cedar or pine work well and resist rot reasonably.
2. **Drill the entrance hole.** Position it high on the front panel, away from the floor, using a hole saw or spade bit sized for your target species.
3. **Add ventilation and drainage.** Small gaps near the roofline and a few drilled holes in the floor keep the birdhouse dry and ventilated.
4. **Assemble the box.** Glue and screw the sides, front, back, and floor together, leaving the roof removable or hinged for seasonal cleaning.
5. **Attach the roof.** Angle it slightly for water runoff, and consider a small overhang to keep rain away from the entrance.
6. **Skip painting the interior.** Leave the inside untreated — paints and stains can be harmful to birds; exterior-only finishing is safest.

## Common Mistakes

- Making the entrance hole too large, which invites predators or competing species.
- Forgetting drainage holes, leading to a damp, unhealthy nesting box.
- Painting or treating the interior, which isn't bird-safe.
